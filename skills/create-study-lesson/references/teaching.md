# Teaching and assessment review rubric

Before finalizing, evaluate the lesson against these questions, and repair weaknesses rather than declaring them acceptable:

- Completeness: can every important source concept, definition, mechanism, classification, exception and practical/clinical detail be located in a teaching section? Have all captions and visual relationships been explained? Are source caveats intact?
- Progression: does the lesson build from known ideas to new ones, explain causal steps, and use examples to resolve likely misconceptions? Would a learner be able to reason through an unfamiliar application?
- Fidelity: are original source claims identifiable through citations? Are supplementary clinical updates/corrections explicitly labeled with consulted evidence, rather than silently rewriting the lecturer?
- Density: does every paragraph earn its place? Remove repeated transitions, generic advice and lists that never explain relationships. Use comparisons and mnemonics only when they aid understanding.
- Media: do diagrams encode meaningful mechanisms/structure? Are images actually accessible, accurately captioned and credited? Are video timestamps verified from inspected content? Do not equate a successful HTTP response with educational relevance.
- Original assessments: were these retrieved from an authorized actual source? Do lecture numbers, subject, scope and question content match? Are text, options, attribution, locators and order preserved? Are the proposed answers independently solved and evidence-checked? Explain important distractors; report ambiguous/disputed keys. Do not reconstruct questions from memory and label them original.
- Checkpoint placement: has everything needed for the question been taught before the checkpoint? Keep source order even when that requires waiting until multiple sections have been taught. Supplement only important coverage gaps.
- Flashcards: do they cover the full concept inventory and test one clear idea each? Are answers short but sufficient? Remove duplicates, vague prompts, overly broad essays and trivial cards. Use exact matching existing decks and don't overwrite user edits.
- Language: are lesson prose, definitions and lookup translations appropriate for this learner? Preserve technical English terms when useful, with accurate Arabic or other-language lookup explanations rather than awkward forced translation.
- Functionality: are there real quiz/flashcard blocks with saved native IDs and relationships? In a published lesson, do the quizzes submit/score/show explanations and cards flip/navigate? Do the glossary terms look up? State any unverified live behavior.

Sources and media can be inaccessible; this is a blocker or a reported omission, never permission to fabricate. Structural backend validation cannot establish pedagogical excellence or scientific truth; this review and actual evidence consultation are mandatory client responsibilities.
